# Lit scan: checkpoint dynamics of belief acquisition, probe validity, relapse, LoRA

**2026-09-19 — Sonnet research agent**

Scope: sanity-check the dynamics experiment (dense-checkpoint probes + behaviour + loss + steering, plain vs. warned documents) against existing literature. Skeptical pass — "not found" preferred over guessing.

## Q1. Checkpoint-trajectory studies of belief/fact acquisition

- **"Layer of Truth: Probing Belief Shifts under Continual Pre-Training Poisoning"**, arXiv:2510.26829 [ABS, abstract + summary fetched, not full PDF]. Churina, Chebrolu, Jaidka. Tracks paired fact/counterfact preference across **checkpoints, layers, and model scales** during continual pretraining on graded poison ratios (0–100%). Findings: belief flips are **abrupt, not gradual**, concentrate in **late layers** (29–36 of a 3B model), and are **partially reversible via activation patching** (up to 56.8%). Corrupted beliefs generalize beyond poisoned prompts and degrade commonsense reasoning selectively. The abstract/summary I could retrieve does **not mention comparing labelled (warned) vs. unlabelled poison** — this looks like an open gap this paper doesn't address, which is exactly our warned-vs-plain axis. Should re-verify against full PDF before citing the layer numbers or the "no labelled/unlabelled comparison" claim.
- **Chang et al., "How Do LLMs Acquire Factual Knowledge During Pretraining?"**, arXiv:2406.11813 [ABS]. NeurIPS 2024. Finds a **power-law forgetting curve** over training steps for newly injected facts, a "data scaling paradox" (more pretraining data ≠ better retention of injected facts), and that larger batch size improves retention. Methodologically close (injected-fact tracking over steps) but doesn't use truth probes or warnings — behavioral only (QA accuracy).
- **Zucchet et al., "How do language models learn facts? Dynamics, curricula and hallucinations"**, arXiv:2503.21676 [ABS]. Reports a **three-phase learning curve with a plateau** before precise fact recall emerges, mechanistically tied to formation of attention-based recall circuits; imbalanced data shortens the plateau; hallucination emerges concurrently with knowledge; fine-tuning new facts in **quickly corrupts existing parametric memory** — directly relevant to why a probe fit once might not transfer, and a candidate mechanism for the plain-vs-warned divergence (competing/overwritten circuits). Related: arXiv:2605.10640 "Towards Understanding Continual Factual Knowledge Acquisition of LMs" (theory+algorithm) turned up adjacent but I did not verify its content — flag as unconfirmed relevance.
- **"Final Checkpoints Are Not Enough"**, arXiv:2607.06648 [ABS]. Turns out to be about **latent chain-of-thought reasoning faithfulness**, not fact/belief acquisition — title matched but topic doesn't transfer directly. Relevant only as a methodological precedent (checkpoint-trajectory + causal patching > single-checkpoint snapshot), not as a substantive result to cite for belief dynamics. Do not cite this for factual claims about knowledge acquisition.
- I found **no paper** that tracks a **linear truth probe's reading across finetuning steps** on the same claim the model is being trained to (mis)believe. Layer of Truth is the closest but is continual-pretraining poisoning at scale, not a small-claim SDF-style setup, and I could not confirm any per-checkpoint probe re-fitting there.

## Q2. Order of acquisition: behaviour vs. representation

No paper directly answers "does output/log-prob move before the probe, or after" in this setting. Anthropic's own alignment blog post, **"Modifying LLM Beliefs with Synthetic Document Finetuning"** [ABS, blog not arXiv — non-peer-reviewed, treat as ABS-equivalent], is the closest analogue to our exact paradigm (SDF, truth probes on middle-layer activations, behavioral probes at multiple depths: MCQ, pairwise comparison, open-ended, "generative distinguish"). Their key finding is a **behaviour/representation dissociation**, but not the one we're asking about: models act *consistent with* the false belief (e.g., solving physics with an inverted law) while still being able to *say* the true law is correct under explicit reasoning prompts — i.e., surface metalinguistic knowledge and downstream task behaviour disagree, not "probe leads/lags output." No checkpoint-level tracking is described. Zucchet et al. (Q1) note hallucination and knowledge "emerge simultaneously," which is suggestive that association and probe-level shift may not separate cleanly, but this is at the fact-injection level, not warned-negation. **Verdict: genuinely open question, not addressed by anything I found.**

## Q3. Probe validity across finetuning/LoRA checkpoints

No paper was found that specifically validates truth-probe transfer across LoRA finetuning checkpoints with recommended controls. Adjacent findings: work on LoRA representation drift (found via search, not independently verified — flag "ID unconfirmed" for exact papers) reports that the **first layer where a LoRA-adapted model's hidden states diverge from the frozen base moves earlier as training progresses**, i.e., drift is nonuniform across layers and grows over steps — meaning a probe fit at step 0 may increasingly mismeasure the finetuned representation geometry at exactly the layers it was trained on. Shuttleworth et al., **"LoRA vs Full Fine-tuning: An Illusion of Equivalence"**, arXiv:2410.21228 [ABS], is the most load-bearing finding here: LoRA-updated weight matrices grow new **"intruder dimensions"** — high-ranking singular vectors nearly orthogonal to the pretrained spectrum — that full finetuning does not produce, and these drive forgetting; a follow-up, **"The Intruder Threshold"**, arXiv:2607.23711 [ABS], gives a spectral law for when they appear. This is a direct pitfall for us: if intruder dimensions appear partway through training, a probe direction fit on the base model may stop being the "same" direction in a meaningful geometric sense even if raw accuracy looks stable.

## Q4. Relapse / recovery after finetuning or unlearning

- **NN paper's own §5** [FULL — fetched primary source]: Phase 1 trains under a soft KL-style constraint that suppresses belief (holds belief rate at 6%); removing the constraint in Phase 2 lets belief rate rise to 48% under continued finetuning on the same negated documents — rapid, substantial, claim-dependent relapse. Confirmed directly from arXiv:2605.13829 html. Importantly, **the NN paper does not itself use linear probes or track checkpoints during Phase 1/2** — so our proposed "probe never changed" explanation would be a genuine addition, not a replication.
- **Lo, Cohen, Barez, "Large Language Models Relearn Removed Concepts"**, arXiv:2401.01814 [ABS]. Concept-pruning based unlearning: pruned concepts are **relocated to earlier layers and reallocate to neurons with similar semantics**, allowing fast reacquisition — a mechanistic story for relapse (the representation moves, doesn't vanish), but for pruning-based unlearning, not warning-suppressed SDF belief.
- **"Unlearning Isn't Deletion: Investigating Reversibility of Machine Unlearning in LLMs"**, arXiv:2505.16831 [ABS]. Gives a **perturbation-based mechanistic account**: distinguishes localized vs. widespread parameter change and four forgetting regimes by reversibility × catastrophicity; representation-level analysis shows internal structure often stays recoverable even when behavioral metrics look like "successful" unlearning. This is the best-matched mechanistic precedent for "the internal representation never actually changed."
- **"Eight Methods to Evaluate Robust Unlearning in LLMs"**, arXiv:2402.16835 [ABS] (Lynch et al.) and **"On Evaluating the Durability of Safeguards for Open-Weight LLMs"**, arXiv:2412.07097 [ABS], and **"Model Tampering Attacks Enable More Rigorous Evaluations of LLM Capabilities"**, arXiv:2502.05209 [ABS] — all converge on: standard unlearning is shallow and reversible with minimal fine-tuning (2412.07097: TAR safeguards undone within 16 steps in 2502.05209's tampering framework), input-space metrics overstate durability, latent/activation-level probing is needed to catch this. This is directly supportive of our activation-steering test being the right kind of check (behavioral metrics alone would miss what's still there internally).

## Q5. LoRA-specific dynamics

- **Biderman et al., "LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less"**, arXiv:2405.09673 [ABS]. LoRA underperforms full FT at fact-learning/memorization in standard low-rank regimes, but forgets less on off-target tasks; behaves like an implicit regularizer, preserves output diversity more than weight decay/dropout.
- **Thinking Machines, "LoRA Without Regret"** (blog, no arXiv ID — flag as non-arXiv, unconfirmed peer review). Claims a "low-regret regime" where properly configured LoRA (all layers **including MLP/MoE experts**, not just attention; LR ~10x full-FT LR; effective batch size kept small, <32) matches full-FT sample efficiency and final performance. Directly actionable for the run's LoRA config: rank-32 applied broadly, and MoE experts specifically called out as needing LoRA coverage, not just router/attention.
- **Shuttleworth et al.** (Q3, arXiv:2410.21228) and **"The Intruder Threshold"** (arXiv:2607.23711 [ABS]) — intruder dimensions are LoRA-specific (not found in full FT), and there's a spectral threshold predicting when they emerge from update strength — a candidate confound if the two runs (plain vs. warned) push different update magnitudes and cross the threshold at different checkpoints.
- **MoE-specific LoRA rank convention**: no single fixed convention found. Search turned up **multiple competing recent proposals** (DR-LoRA: dynamic per-expert rank via saliency scores; HiLo: joint expert-count + per-layer rank tuning; a result that multi-expert LoRA-MoE is mathematically equivalent to a single LoRA whose rank is block-partitioned) — none of these is clearly "the standard," so whatever the pod-side implementation does (uniform rank-32 per expert, presumably) is a judgment call, not literature-backed default. Flag for the writeup: state explicitly what convention was used and that it's not a settled choice in the field.

## VERDICT

- **(a) Checkpoint-level comparison of labelled-false vs. unlabelled synthetic-document finetuning, with probe + behaviour + loss decomposition:** **OPEN.** Nothing found does this combination. Layer of Truth is the nearest neighbor (checkpoints × probe × patching) but at pretraining-poisoning scale without a labelled/unlabelled (warned/plain) axis, and the NN paper itself has no checkpoints or probes.
- **(b) Activation steering at each checkpoint to test whether predicting the warning depends on the truth representation:** **OPEN.** No paper combines per-checkpoint steering with a warning-prediction target of this kind. Closest analogues are single-snapshot tampering/steering evaluations (2502.05209, TAR-related work) — none do it across a training trajectory.
- **(c) Explaining relapse by showing the internal claim representation never changed:** **PARTIALLY CLAIMED.** The mechanistic shape of this claim is anticipated by arXiv:2505.16831 ("Unlearning Isn't Deletion" — representations recoverable despite behavioral suppression) and arXiv:2401.01814 (relocated/reallocated representations enable fast relearning), but neither is in the SDF/negation-neglect setting, and neither uses a continuously-tracked linear probe through the suppression-then-relapse cycle. Doing this for NN's specific Phase 1→2 transition, with a probe held constant across the transition, appears to be a new instantiation of a known general phenomenon rather than a novel mechanism.

## Pitfalls and controls to add (cheap)

1. **Re-fit (or at least re-validate) the probe at multiple checkpoints**, not just the base model — given intruder-dimension and drift evidence (Q3), a fixed base-model probe direction may silently stop tracking the same concept partway through training. Cheap control: at a handful of checkpoints, check probe accuracy on a frozen held-out control set of unrelated true/false statements, and report if accuracy degrades even where claim-probe readings shift.
2. **Add an unrelated control-claim probe** (a fact untouched by finetuning) at every checkpoint, so any drift in probe readings on the target claim can be compared against drift on a claim that shouldn't move — separates "probe geometry rotted" from "belief representation actually changed."
3. **Report per-layer, not just one layer** — Layer of Truth found effects concentrated in specific late layers; if the probe is single-layer, a null result could be a wrong-layer artifact rather than "no representational shift."
4. **Check for intruder dimensions / spectral shift** in the LoRA update matrices at a few checkpoints (cheap SVD on the adapter deltas) — if plain vs. warned runs cross the "intruder threshold" (arXiv:2607.23711) at different points, that's a confound for any plain-vs-warned comparison of dynamics, not just a curiosity.
5. **State and justify the MoE LoRA-rank convention explicitly** (Q5) — literature has no settled default; note whether rank-32 is applied per-expert uniformly, and flag this as a design choice, not a citation-backed one.
6. **Smoke-test the steering intervention's sign/magnitude before the full checkpoint sweep** — given the "single-driver / smoke test" project rule already in place, extend it explicitly to the steering component: confirm the steering vector at one checkpoint produces the expected warning-prediction degradation before running it across all checkpoints.
7. **Distinguish "abrupt" from "gradual" carefully in the writeup** — Layer of Truth's headline result is abrupt phase-transition-like belief flips; if our dense-checkpoint (every step for first 128) data shows something gradual, that is itself a finding worth flagging as a discrepancy with the closest prior work, not silently smoothed over.
8. **Track first-warning vs. later/copyable-warning loss separately over training**, not just at the end — if warning-loss and claim-loss trajectories diverge early, that bears directly on the "order of acquisition" question (Q2), which no existing work answers; this is a place our design can generate genuinely new evidence cheaply, from data we're already collecting.

## Surprises

1. The NN paper itself (2605.13829) uses **no linear probes and no checkpoint tracking at all** in either its main results or its §5 relapse analysis — everything there is judged purely by belief-rate and loss at the end of each phase. Our design is not incrementally extending their method, it's the first mechanistic pass on their own central finding.
2. "Final Checkpoints Are Not Enough" (2607.06648) is a near-perfect title match for our framing but is about a completely different research question (latent CoT faithfulness) — a reminder that title-based literature matching for this scan needs full-text confirmation, not abstract-level pattern matching.
3. There is no settled convention for applying LoRA rank inside MoE expert layers in the field at all — multiple 2025/2026 papers are actively proposing competing schemes (uniform, saliency-based dynamic, hierarchical), so whatever the pod script does for Qwen3.5's MoE layers is not backed by an established default and should be stated as a choice.
