# Literature scan: does old+new fact coexistence after edit/finetuning predate our finding?

2026-09-18, Sonnet research agent

Question: does the field already show that gradient finetuning on a contradicting fact leaves the *original* fact internally readable as true (linear probe) while outputs prefer the new fact — and does anyone connect this to negation/label failure? Web search + full-text fetch where noted.

## 1. Knowledge editing: does the original fact survive?

- **"Suppressed, Not Erased"** (arXiv:2609.18985) [FULL, via WebFetch]. Trains a linear "trace probe" on GPT-2-XL hidden states after ROME/constrained-fine-tuning/GRACE edits (CounterFact, n=50). Original object stays linearly decodable well above chance post-edit (ROME 0.96, constrained FT 0.86, GRACE 0.79, chance 0.50). Because GRACE changes *zero* base weights, they argue the surviving trace isn't an artifact of incomplete weight change — it's a property of the representation. Directly analogous method to ours, but on ROME/GRACE, not standard SDF-style gradient finetuning of a whole model.
- **"Exposing the Illusion of Erasure in Knowledge Editing for LLMs"** (arXiv:2606.23276) [ABS]. Edited facts "continue to surface"; frames ROME/MEMIT-style low-rank updates as suppression/redistribution in representation space, not removal; original knowledge sits in "narrow, anisotropic regions" recoverable by adversarial prompts. No confirmed use of a linear truth probe in what I could read; doesn't clearly cover ordinary gradient finetuning.
- **"One Mask to Rule Them All: On Hidden Facts after Editing and How to Find Them"** (arXiv:2605.28839) [ABS]. A learned binary mask reverses 70-80% of ROME/MEMIT-style edits, i.e. a shared functional structure (overattention) suppresses retrieval of the original fact without deleting it — same "suppress don't erase" story, different mechanism (attention overattention vs. weight edit).
- **"Has this Fact been Edited? Detecting Knowledge Edits in Language Models"** (arXiv:2405.02765) [ABS, snippet only]. Detects that a fact *was* edited from the representation — consistent with edits leaving a detectable residue, though this is about detecting the edit event, not recovering the pre-edit content.

## 2. Finetuning/continued pretraining: do old and new facts coexist?

- **"Right Knowledge, Wrong Answer: Characterizing Parametric Temporal Conflict"** (arXiv:2606.20959) [ABS/partial]. Studies naturally-arising temporal conflicts in pretrained models (Wikidata position-holder transitions, n=8,746): "the newer fact is present and recoverable, but the default forward pass prefers the outdated one." Activation patching flips the answer in 72-85% of cases; date-prefix prompting recovers the newer fact 61-81% of the time. This is the closest published analogue to our pattern — but it's about naturally-occurring pretraining-era conflicts, not deliberate contradicting-fact finetuning, and I could not confirm whether they use a linear truth probe vs. patching/prompting only.
- **"Does Fine-Tuning LLMs on New Knowledge Encourage Hallucinations?"** (Gekhman et al., arXiv:2405.05904) [ABS]. Shows FT-introduced new facts are learned slowly and increase hallucination on unrelated pre-existing facts; frames pretraining as the source of factual knowledge and FT as behavior-shaping. Doesn't probe internal representations for old/new coexistence — output-level only, as far as I could confirm.
- **"The Law of Knowledge Overshadowing"** (arXiv:2502.16143) [ABS] and **"Knowledge Overshadowing Causes Amalgamated Hallucination"** (arXiv:2407.08039) [ABS]. Dominant knowledge "obscures" (not erases) less prominent knowledge during generation; scales with popularity/length/model size. Same overshadow-not-overwrite flavor, but framed around generation errors, not truth-probe readout of a specific implanted counterfactual.
- No paper found that runs a linear truth probe before/after ordinary (non-localized) gradient finetuning on a single contradicting fact and reports probe truth-values for *both* old and new facts simultaneously — this specific setup looks genuinely uncovered.

## 3. Machine unlearning: residual/recoverable "unlearned" knowledge

- **Lynch et al., "Eight Methods to Evaluate Robust Unlearning in LLMs"** (arXiv:2402.16835) [ABS]. Proposes 8 evaluation axes including "latent knowledge" and relearning-via-fine-tuning; found persistent similarity in latent representations of "unlearned" models — i.e., unlearning is shallow/suppressive, similar spirit to our result but for deletion rather than contradiction.
- **"Does your LLM truly unlearn? An embarrassingly simple approach to recover unlearned knowledge"** (arXiv:2410.16454) [ABS]. Recovers "deleted" knowledge via simple techniques (quantization, light relearning), reinforcing suppression-not-erasure.
- **"Unlearning Isn't Deletion: Investigating Reversibility of Machine Unlearning in LLMs"** (arXiv:2505.16831) [ABS]. Title states the thesis directly; relearning on forget/retain/unrelated data all realign suppressed features, meaning "unlearned" content lives on beneath a suppression layer.
- **Patil et al., "Can Sensitive Information Be Deleted From LLMs?"** (arXiv:2309.17410) [ABS] — confirmed ID. Whitebox attacks recover "deleted" info 38% of the time even after weight-editing deletion attempts; earliest paper in this cluster (2023), establishes the pattern later work builds on.

## 4. Synthetic-document finetuning specifically

- **Slocum et al., "Believe It or Not: How Deeply do LLMs Believe Implanted Facts?"** (arXiv:2510.17941) [FULL via HTML fetch]. This is the closest existing work and it is **directly informative and partially contrary to our hypothesis**. They train truth probes and find SDF makes probes read the implanted false claim as true — **and** (their §4.3) "not only do these methods cause the model to represent the implanted fact as true, but they also cause it to represent the original, reference belief as **false**." An adversarial probe (trained to separate implanted-false from genuine facts) is fooled only for the most plausible categories (AKC/all, BKC 3/5) — elsewhere it correctly flags implanted facts as not-genuine. So in their setup revision (old→false), not mere addition, is the more common probe outcome, though the most-plausible-fact subset is where genuine coexistence/indistinguishability shows up. They don't test negation-in-training-text specifically; robustness tests are adversarial system prompts, multi-turn debate, critique tasks — SDF beliefs are stable against all of these.
- **Wang et al., "Modifying LLM Beliefs with Synthetic Document Finetuning"** — this is an **Anthropic Alignment Science blog/LessWrong post** (alignment.anthropic.com/2025/modifying-beliefs-via-sdf/; LessWrong id ARQs7KYY9vJHeYsGc), not an arXiv preprint. ID unconfirmed — treat as non-peer-reviewed source; cite as blog post, not paper.
- **"Shallow Beliefs: SDF does not inoculate against emergent misalignment from reward hacking"** (arXiv:2609.14998) [ABS]. Found via search, useful and on-topic though not in your original list: SDF "can predictably steer downstream generalization when inserting new associations, but struggles and has unpredictable effects when overriding existing associations" — i.e. their own framing distinguishes add vs. override, and suggests overriding is where SDF is weakest/most surprising, consistent with our "adds, doesn't reconcile" idea, though again their probe evidence (via Slocum, same lab cluster) leans toward the old fact flipping to false rather than staying true.

## 5. "Training adds, doesn't reconcile" / competing-memory-readout framing

- **Ortu et al., "Competition of Mechanisms: Tracing How Language Models Handle Facts and Counterfactuals"** (arXiv:2402.11655, ACL 2024) [ABS]. GPT-2 mechanistic study: attention heads in later layers arbitrate between parametric factual recall and in-context counterfactual info; this is the standard cite for "how a model picks between two candidate answers," but it's about context-vs-parameters, not two competing *parametric* facts from sequential training.
- **"How Do Language Models Choose Between Context and Memory?"** (arXiv:2609.00753) [ABS]. Same theme, more recent; parametric and contextual circuits are largely distinct and "coexist as superposed signals rather than competing via direct suppression" — nice mechanistic vocabulary but again context-vs-parameter, not old-parametric-vs-new-parametric.
- No paper found using the literal phrase "training adds rather than overwrites" or "facts are appended not revised." This framing, applied specifically to *sequential finetuning writing a second parametric fact beside a first*, appears to be an open articulation gap even though the underlying phenomenon (suppression not erasure) is well established for editing and unlearning.

## VERDICT

- **(a)** "After gradient finetuning on a contradicting fact, the original fact remains represented as TRUE by a linear probe while outputs prefer the new fact" — **PARTIALLY CLAIMED**. Suppression-not-erasure is well established for *localized editing* (ROME/GRACE, arXiv:2609.18985) and for *natural* temporal conflict (arXiv:2606.20959, recoverable-but-not-preferred). But for *ordinary whole-model gradient finetuning* specifically, and with the probe reading the old fact as still-TRUE (not merely recoverable via patching/prompting), I found no direct precedent.
- **(b)** Same, in the SDF/belief-implantation setting specifically — **PARTIALLY CLAIMED, and actively complicated by counter-evidence**. Slocum et al. (2510.17941) directly tested this and found the more common outcome is the *opposite* of ours: the probe reads the original reference fact as **false** post-SDF, not true. Coexistence (probe can't tell implanted from genuine) only shows up for their most-plausible fact categories. Our preliminary Olympics/Vesuvius result — old fact still reads true — should be treated as a genuine point of tension with existing work, not a confirmation of it; worth flagging prominently and re-checking for confounds (probe calibration, checkpoint/model differences, fact plausibility) before treating it as new evidence for "adds not reconciles."
- **(c)** Coexistence explains why negation/fiction labels fail to gate belief (write step adds content, never reconciles with labels) — **OPEN**. No paper connects representational coexistence/suppression mechanisms to the specific failure of epistemic-qualifier labels in training text. This causal link (write-then-no-reconciliation as the mechanism behind Negation Neglect) appears to be the paper's actual novel contribution.

## Surprises

- Slocum et al. (arXiv:2510.17941) is not just relevant background — it ran essentially the same probe experiment we're running, on the same SDF paradigm, and got a result (old fact flips to false) that cuts against our preliminary finding. This needs to be addressed head-on, not filed as "related work."
- The "suppression not erasure" result is remarkably consistent across three unrelated subfields (editing, unlearning, temporal-conflict) but nobody has yet applied it to explain why in-training epistemic qualifiers (negation, "this is fiction") fail — that gap looks real and is the paper's opening.
- 2609.18985's use of GRACE (a weight-frozen, retrieval-based editor) to show the trace persists even with *zero* weight change is a strong independent argument that "coexistence" is about representational geometry, not merely incomplete gradient updates — useful for the mechanism section regardless of which direction our own probe result nets out to.
